# Ecclesiasticus 

## Chapter 26


Of good and bad women.




      <BibleText verse={1}>Happy is the husband of a good wife: for the number of his years is double.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={2}>A virtuous woman rejoiceth her husband, and shall fulfil the years of his life in peace.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={3}>A good wife is a good portion, she shall be given in the portion of them that fear God, to a man for his good deeds.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={4}>Rich or poor, if his heart is good, his countenance shall be cheerful at all times.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={5}>Of three things my heart hath been afraid, and at the fourth my face hath trembled:</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={6}>The accusation of a city, and the gathering together of the people:</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={7}>And a false calumny, all are more grievous than death.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={8}>A jealous woman is the grief and mourning of the heart.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={9}>With a jealous woman is a scourge of the tongue which communicateth with all.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={10}>As a yoke of oxen that is moved to and fro, so also is a wicked woman: he that hath hold of her, is as he that taketh hold of a scorpion.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={11}>A drunken woman is a great wrath: and her reproach and shame shall not be hid.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={12}>The fornication of a woman shall be known by the haughtiness of her eyes and by her eyelids.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={13}>On a daughter that turneth not away herself, set a strict watch: lest finding an opportunity she abuse herself.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={14}>Take heed of the impudence of her eyes, and wonder not if she slight thee.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={15}>She will open her mouth as a thirsty traveller to the fountain, and will drink of every water near her, and will sit down by every hedge, and open her quiver against every arrow, until she fail.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={16}>The grace of a diligent woman shall delight her husband, and shall fat his bones.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={17}>Her discipline is the gift of God.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={18}>Such is a wise and silent woman, and there is nothing so much worth as a well instructed soul.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={19}>A holy and shamefaced woman is grace upon grace.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={20}>And no price is worthy of a continent soul.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={21}>As the sun when it riseth to the world in the high places of God, so is the beauty of a good wife for the ornament of her house.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={22}>As the lamp shining upon the holy candlestick, so is the beauty of the face in a ripe age,</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={23}>As golden pillars upon bases of silver, so are the firm feet upon the soles of a steady woman.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={24}>As everlasting foundations upon a solid rock, so the commandments of God in the heart of a holy woman.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={25}>At two things my heart is grieved, and the third bringeth anger upon me.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={26}>A man of war fainting through poverty, and a man of sense despised:</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={27}>And he that passeth over from justice to sin, God hath prepared such an one for the sword.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={28}>Two sorts of callings have appeared to me hard and dangerous: a merchant is hardly free from negligence: and a huckster shall not be justified from the sins of the lips.</BibleText>

:::note
Note 1: From negligence...That is, from the neglect of the service of God: because the eager pursuit of the mammon of this world, is apt to make men of that calling forget the great duties of loving God above all 
Note 2: things, and their neighbours as themselves.--Ibid. A huckster...Or, a retailer of wine. Men of that profession are both greatly exposed to danger of sin themselves, and are too often accessary to the sins of others. 
:::
