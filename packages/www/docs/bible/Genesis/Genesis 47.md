# Genesis 

## Chapter 47


Jacob and his sons are presented before Pharao:  he giveth them the land of Gessen.  The famine forceth the Egyptians to sell all their possessions to the king.




      <BibleText verse={1}>Then Joseph went in and told Pharao, saying: My father and brethren, their sheep and their herds, and all that they possess, are come out of the land of Chanaan: and behold they stay in the land of Gessen.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={2}>Five men also, the last of his brethren, he presented before the king:</BibleText>

:::note
Note 1: The last...xtremos. Some interpret this word of the chiefest, and most rightly: but Joseph seems rather to have chosen out such as had the meanest appearance, that Pharao might not think of employing them at court, with danger of their morals and religion. 
:::

      <BibleText verse={3}>And he asked them: What is your occupation? They answered: We, thy servants, are shepherds, both we and our fathers.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={4}>We are come to sojourn in thy land, because there is no grass for the flocks of thy servants, the famine being very grievous in the land of Chanaan: and we pray thee to give orders that we thy servants may be in the land of Gessen.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={5}>The king therefore said to Joseph: Thy father and thy brethren are come to thee.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={6}>The land of Egypt is before thee: and make them dwell in the best place, and give them the land of Gessen. And if thou knowest that there are industrious men among them, make them rulers over my cattle.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={7}>After this Joseph brought in his father to the king, and presented him before him: and he blessed him.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={8}>And being asked by him: How many are the days of the years of thy life?</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={9}>He answered: The days of my pilgrimage are a hundred and thirty years, few, and evil, and they are not come up to the days of the pilgrimage of my fathers.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={10}>And blessing the king, he went out.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={11}>But Joseph gave a possession to his father and his brethren in Egypt, in the best place of the land, in Ramesses, as Pharao had commanded.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={12}>And he nourished them, and all his father's house, allowing food to every one.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={13}>For in the whole world there was want of bread, and a famine had oppressed the land, more especially of Egypt and Chanaan;</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={14}>Out of which he gathered up all the money for the corn which they bought, and brought it in to the king's treasure.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={15}>And when the buyers wanted money, all Egypt came to Joseph, saying: Give us bread: why should we die in thy presence, having now no money?</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={16}>And he answered them: Bring me your cattle, and for them I will give you food, if you have no money.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={17}>And when they had brought them, he gave them food in exchange for their horses, and sheep, and oxen, and asses: and he maintained them that year for the exchange of their cattle.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={18}>And they came the second year, and said to him: We will not hide from our lord, how that our money is spent, and our cattle also are gone: neither art thou ignorant that we have nothing now left but our bodies and our lands.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={19}>Why therefore shall we die before thy eyes? we will be thine, both we and our lands: buy us to be the king's servants, and give us seed, lest for want of tillers the land be turned into a wilderness.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={20}>So Joseph bought all the land of Egypt, every man selling his possessions, because of the greatness of the famine. And he brought it into Pharao's hands:</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={21}>And all its people from one end of the borders of Egypt, even to the other end thereof,</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={22}>Except the land of the priests, which had been given them by the king: to whom also a certain allowance of food was given out of the public stores, and therefore they were not forced to sell their possessions.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={23}>Then Joseph said to the people: Behold, as you see, both you and your lands belong to Pharao; take seed and sow the fields,</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={24}>That you may have corn. The fifth part you shall give to the king; the other four you shall have for seed, and for food for your families and children.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={25}>And they answered: our life is in thy hand; only let my lord look favourably upon us, and we will gladly serve the king.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={26}>From that time unto this day, in the whole land of Egypt, the fifth part is paid to the kings, and it is become as a law, except the land of the priests, which was free from this covenant.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={27}>So Israel dwelt in Egypt, that is, in the land of Gessen, and possessed it; and grew, and was multiplied exceedingly.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={28}>And he lived in it seventeen years: and all the days of his life came to a hundred and forty-seven years.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={29}>And when he saw that the day of his death drew nigh, he called his son Joseph, and said to him: If I have found favour in thy sight, put thy hand under my thigh; and thou shalt shew me this kindness and truth, not to bury me in Egypt.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={30}>But I will sleep with my fathers, and thou shalt take me away out of this land, and bury me in the burying place of my ancestors. And Joseph answered him: I will do what thou hast commanded.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={31}>And he said: Swear then to me. And as he was swearing, Israel adored God, turning to the bed's head.</BibleText>

:::note
Note 1: To the bed's head...St. Paul, Heb. 11.21, following the Greek translation of the Septuagint, reads adored the top of his rod. Where note, that the same word in the Hebrew, according to the different pointing of it, signifies both a bed and a rod. And to verify both these sentences, we must understand that Jacob leaning on Joseph's rod adored, turning towards the head of his bed: which adoration, inasmuch as it was referred to God, was an absolute and sovereign worship: but inasmuch as it was referred to the rod of Joseph, as a figure of the sceptre, that is, of the royal dignity of Christ, was only an inferior and relative honour. 
:::
