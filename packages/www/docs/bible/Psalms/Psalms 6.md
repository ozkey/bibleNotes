# Psalms 

## Chapter 6


Domine, ne in furore. A prayer of a penitent sinner, under the scourge of God.  The first penitential psalm.




      <BibleText verse={1}>Unto the end, in verses, a psalm for David, for the octave.</BibleText>

:::note
Note 1: For the octave...That is, to be sung on an instrument of eight strings. St. Augustine understands it mystically, of the last resurrection, and the world to come; which is, as it were, the octave, or eighth day, after the seven days of this mortal life: and for this octave, sinners must dispose themselves, like David, by bewailing their sins, whilst they are here upon earth. 
:::

      <BibleText verse={2}>O Lord, rebuke me not in thy indignation, nor chastise me in thy wrath.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={3}>Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak: heal me, O Lord, for my bones are troubled.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={4}>And my soul is troubled exceedingly: but thou, O Lord, how long?</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={5}>Turn to me, O Lord, and deliver my soul: O save me for thy mercy's sake.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={6}>For there is no one in death, that is mindful of thee: and who shall confess to thee in hell?</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={7}>I have laboured in my groanings, every night I will wash my bed: I will water my couch with my tears.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={8}>My eye is troubled through indignation: I have grown old amongst all my enemies.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={9}>Depart from em, all ye workers of iniquity: for the Lord hath heard the voice of my weeping.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={10}>The Lord hath heard my supplication: the Lord hath received my prayer.</BibleText>


      <BibleText verse={11}>Let all my enemies be ashamed, and be very much troubled: let them be turned back, and be ashamed very speedily.</BibleText>
